## Conditions and standard

This examination is **untimed and self-paced**. Attempt it closed-book without a calculator. Use the course prerequisites and Articles 1.1–2.8. Give complete reasoning, retain all domains and exclusions, and prefer exact answers. Proofs must establish every asserted case and every claimed equality condition.

There are exactly **30 free-response problems: 15 Medium and 15 Hard**. Each is worth 10 points, for 300 total. The mastery threshold is **270/300 (90%)**. An unsupported answer does not demonstrate mastery. After marking, revisit the relevant articles and correct incomplete arguments before progressing.

## Medium

1. Real numbers $x,y$ satisfy $x+y=5$ and $xy=4$. Find $x^4+y^4$ without solving for $x,y$ individually. Explain why the supplied data are consistent over the reals.
2. Factor $x^4+4x^3-x^2-10x+4$ into two monic real quadratic polynomials by identifying a repeated expression. Determine all real roots exactly.
3. Simplify $\dfrac{x^2-9}{x^2-x-6}\div\dfrac{x+3}{x-2}$. State its full original domain, and determine whether the original expression can equal zero.
4. Solve $2/(x-1)+3/(x+1)=2$ over $\mathbb R$, retaining exclusions.
5. Solve $\sqrt{3x+4}=x+2$, with a justification that every retained candidate satisfies the original equation.
6. Solve $x^{4/3}-5x^{2/3}+4=0$ using the course's real rational-power convention.
7. Solve $|2x+1|+|x-4|=10$, with exhaustive sign cases.
8. A service point is to be placed at coordinate $t$ on a straight road with two stations at $-4$ and $2$. Minimize the larger of the two travel distances, giving the unique location and a proof of optimality.
9. Solve $(x^2+x-6)/(x^2-4)\le0$, explaining how cancellation affects the domain.
10. Solve $1/(x-2)\ge1/(2x+1)$ by sign analysis.
11. Nonnegative side lengths $a,b$ obey $a+2b=12$. Find the greatest value of $ab^2$ and every attaining pair, using a proved inequality rather than calculus.
12. Real $x,y$ have $x+y=7$. Find the least value of $x^2/2+y^2/5$ and the unique attaining pair.
13. Solve $(a+2)x=a^2-4$ for every real parameter $a$.
14. Classify the number of distinct real roots of $x^2-2tx+4=0$ for every real $t$, and give the roots when they exist.
15. For all $u,v\ge0$, prove $|\sqrt u-\sqrt v|\le\sqrt{|u-v|}$ and characterize every equality case.

## Hard

16. Determine all ordered real pairs $(x,y)$ satisfying $(x+y)^2=9$ and $x^4+y^4=17$. Explain why algebraically possible values of $xy$ need not all correspond to real pairs.
17. Prove that $n^4+n^2+1$ has at least two distinct prime divisors for every integer $n\ge2$. Your argument must establish coprimality of suitable nontrivial factors, rather than assuming each factor is prime.
18. Solve $\dfrac1{x+1}+\dfrac1{x+2}=\dfrac1{x+3}+\dfrac1{x+4}$. Seek a useful regrouping, and verify your complete real solution set against the original exclusions.
19. Solve $\sqrt{x+6}+\sqrt{x-3}=\sqrt{4x+3}$. Justify every squaring and check the original equation.
20. Solve $\sqrt{x+1}/(x-1)\le1$. Treat the sign of the denominator before any squaring.
21. Solve $|x+3|-|x-2|\ge x$. Include all breakpoints and justify the possibly disconnected solution set.
22. Classify the number of distinct real solutions of $|x-2|=t|x+1|$ for every real $t$, and give all solutions.
23. Solve $(x-1)/(x-t)>1$ for every real $t$. Then determine exactly which $t$ admit at least one solution in $[-1,1]$.
24. Determine all real $t$ for which $x^2-2tx+t+3\ge0$ for every $x\ge0$. Give every pair $(t,x)$ attaining equality within the permitted region.
25. Determine the smallest real constant $C$ for which $(a-2b+3c)^2\le C(a^2+b^2+c^2)$ whenever real $a+b+c=0$. Prove sharpness and give every equality triple.
26. Positive $x,y,z$ satisfy $x+y+z=6$. Prove $\dfrac1{x+y}+\dfrac4{y+z}+\dfrac9{z+x}>3$. Prove also that no constant greater than $3$ can replace $3$ in this universal strict inequality: for every $\varepsilon>0$, construct a permitted triple for which the sum is less than $3+\varepsilon$. Do not use calculus or an unproved limit rule.
27. Positive real numbers $a,b,c$ satisfy $abc=1$ and $a+b+c=a^2+b^2+c^2$. Determine all such triples and prove completeness.
28. Solve $(t-1)x^2-2tx+t+1=0$ for every real $t$. Then determine exactly which parameters give one positive solution and no negative solution, allowing a zero solution in addition.
29. Determine all real $t$ such that $x^2+y^2+2txy-2x-2y+2\ge0$ for every real pair $(x,y)$. Prove necessity and sufficiency, and classify equality for every admissible $t$.
30. Solve $|x^2-4|=t(x-2)$ for every real $t$. Preserve solutions at which cancellation would be invalid, and state the distinct-solution count in every parameter case.
