## Objective and preparation

Establish the logical and mathematical foundations required for rigorous problem-solving. Prerequisites are **Algebra I, Algebra II, and Euclidean Geometry**; no calculus is assumed.

By the end of the unit, you should be able to:

- Distinguish valid mathematical arguments from invalid reasoning.
- Translate mathematical statements into precise logical language.
- Construct elementary proofs using appropriate techniques.
- Apply mathematical induction to identities and inequalities.
- Identify hidden assumptions and construct counterexamples.

## Practice and mastery

Each article contains exactly 20 free-response problems: 6 Easy, 6 Medium, 6 Hard, and 2 Olympiad. Attempt them independently before consulting the final answers at the end of that article. Proof answers give only conclusions or essential ideas.

The unit examination has 30 problems, exclusively Medium and Hard. It is untimed and self-paced; a closed-book attempt without a calculator is recommended. Full reasoning is required. The mastery threshold is 90% (270/300), followed by correction and review of weaknesses. Its [answer key](./examination/answer-key/) is separate from the questions.
